Homemade Sandwich Bread

By Harley  ·  From My Kitchen

This is the loaf I make every week. Soft, fluffy, slices perfectly, and holds up to anything you put on it. Once I started making my own sandwich bread I could not go back to the store — the ingredient list on those bags is a mile long and none of it needs to be there. This has seven ingredients and tastes like actual bread.

My kids take sandwiches to school on this bread every single day and I feel good about every single one of them.

What You Need — Makes 1 loaf

  • 3 cups all-purpose flour
  • 2¼ tsp active dry yeast (one packet)
  • 1 tsp salt
  • 1 tbsp sugar
  • 2 tbsp butter, softened
  • 1 cup warm milk
  • ¼ cup warm water

How to Make It

  1. Dissolve the yeast and sugar in the warm water. Let sit 5–10 minutes until foamy. If it doesn't foam, start over — your yeast needs to be active for the bread to rise properly.
  2. In a large bowl, combine the flour and salt. Add the yeast mixture, warm milk, and softened butter. Mix until a dough forms.
  3. Knead on a floured surface for 8–10 minutes until the dough is smooth, soft, and springs back when poked.
  4. Place in a lightly oiled bowl, cover with a clean towel, and let rise in a warm spot for 1 hour until doubled.
  5. Punch the dough down and shape it into a log roughly the length of your loaf pan. Place it seam-side down in a greased 9x5 inch loaf pan.
  6. Cover and let rise for 45 minutes until the dough crowns about an inch above the top of the pan.
  7. Preheat your oven to 375°F. Bake for 30–35 minutes until deep golden brown on top. It should sound hollow when you tap the bottom.
  8. Cool on a wire rack for at least 30 minutes before slicing. I know it's hard to wait but slicing too early makes the inside gummy.

Harley's tip: Warm milk gives you a softer, richer loaf than water alone. Don't skip it. And if your kitchen is cold, let the dough rise in the oven with just the oven light on — it creates the perfect warm environment without any heat.

This bread stays fresh on the counter for 3–4 days wrapped in a clean towel or stored in a bread bag. For longer storage, slice the whole loaf and freeze it — then you can pull out exactly what you need and toast it straight from frozen. Best thing ever for busy school mornings.
