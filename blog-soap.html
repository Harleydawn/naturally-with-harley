I Switched to Goat Milk Soap — Here's What Happened

By Harley  ·  Kids & Family

Soap was actually the very first thing I swapped when I started going more natural. I figured if I was going to change anything, it made sense to start with what we put on our skin every single day. Then I read the label on what we'd been using and I had one of those moments where you can't unsee something.

So I made the switch. I was skeptical it would make any real difference. Here's what actually happened over 30 days.

Week One: Mostly Adjustment

The first week was quiet. No complaints, no pushback — honestly a smoother transition than I expected. The goat milk soap smelled different, more gentle and subtle, and it lathered a little differently than what we were used to. Nothing dramatic. I was keeping an eye on things but wasn't expecting much yet.

Week Two: Something Shifted

Around week two something clicked. My skin just felt better — calmer, softer, less irritated after washing. I'd never really thought about soap being the problem before, but I was starting to connect the dots. I didn't want to get my hopes up too soon, so I just kept watching.

Week Three: I Was Convinced

By week three I couldn't ignore it anymore. My skin just felt genuinely good — not tight after the shower, not irritated, not like it needed five products to recover. I had normalized all of that for so long. It wasn't normal. It was the soap. Goat milk soap doesn't strip your skin the way conventional soap does — it actually works with your skin's natural barrier instead of fighting it.

What I learned: Goat milk contains lactic acid, which gently exfoliates and helps your skin absorb moisture. It's also naturally rich in vitamins and fatty acids that conventional soap strips away completely.

Week Four: No Going Back

By the end of the month I had thrown out every conventional soap in the house. Not dramatically — I just used them up and didn't replace them. The sensitivity I used to have around my face cleared up too. I don't know if it was directly related, but the timing lined up perfectly.

The thing that got me most was realizing how much I had normalized dryness and irritation. I thought that was just what skin did. It's not. It was a reaction to what I was putting on it every day.

What I Use Now

I switched to a simple goat milk soap and haven't looked back. It's creamy, it rinses clean, and my skin actually feels nourished after a shower instead of tight and stripped. The ingredient list is short, nothing I can't pronounce, and it works better than anything I used before. That's everything I could ask for in a soap.

If you're on the fence about making this swap, just try it for a month. That's all I did. The difference was so clear that it made me want to look at everything else I was using — and that's really how this whole journey started for me.

Bonus: DIY Goat Milk Honey Soap

This is the first soap I ever made at home and honestly it's still my favorite. It takes about fifteen minutes, uses a melt-and-pour base so there's no lye involved, and the result is a creamy, nourishing bar that smells incredible. The honey adds a natural glow and the oatmeal makes it so gentle you can use it on your face.

Goat Milk Honey Soap Recipe:

• 1 lb goat milk melt & pour soap base
• 1 tablespoon raw honey
• 1 tablespoon colloidal oatmeal
• 10 drops lavender essential oil

How to make it:
1. Cut soap base into small chunks and melt in the microwave in 30-second intervals, stirring between each round until fully melted.
2. Let it cool for about 2 minutes so it's no longer steaming.
3. Stir in the honey and oatmeal until fully combined.
4. Add lavender essential oil and stir gently.
5. Pour into silicone soap molds.
6. Let set at room temperature for 4–6 hours.
7. Unmold, wrap in wax paper, and store in a cool dry place.

Makes 4–6 bars depending on mold size. Lasts up to a year unwrapped.

The honey is a natural humectant — it pulls moisture into your skin and keeps it there. The oatmeal soothes and gently exfoliates without being harsh. These bars make the best gifts too. I've given them to everyone I know and they always ask for more.

Bonus: DIY Frankincense & Myrrh Goat Milk Soap Bar

This one is richer and creamier — perfect for winter or anyone whose skin tends to feel tight after washing. The shea butter melts right into the soap base and creates a bar that leaves your skin feeling genuinely soft, not just clean. I make a big batch of these every few months and keep them stocked.

Frankincense & Myrrh Goat Milk Soap Bar Recipe:

• 1 lb goat milk melt & pour soap base
• 1 tablespoon shea butter
• 1 tablespoon sweet almond oil
• 10 drops frankincense essential oil
• 8 drops myrrh essential oil

How to make it:
1. Cut soap base into chunks and melt in the microwave in 30-second intervals, stirring between each until fully melted.
2. While still hot, add the shea butter and stir until completely melted and combined.
3. Stir in the sweet almond oil.
4. Let cool for 2–3 minutes, then add frankincense and myrrh essential oils and stir gently.
5. Pour into soap molds slowly to avoid air bubbles.
6. Let set for 6–8 hours or overnight.
7. Unmold, wrap individually in wax paper, and store away from heat and humidity.

Makes 4–6 bars. The frankincense and myrrh together smell warm, resinous, and completely natural.

Sweet almond oil is one of the best oils for skin — lightweight, absorbs fast, and packed with vitamins. Combined with shea butter and goat milk, this bar genuinely nourishes while it cleans. I use it every single day and my skin has never felt better. Once you make your own soap, buying it from a store starts to feel unnecessary.
