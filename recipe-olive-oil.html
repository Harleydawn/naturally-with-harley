Olive Oil — How to Use It Right

By Harley  ·  From My Kitchen

Good olive oil is one of the single most important ingredients you can have in your kitchen. I use it every single day — to cook, to drizzle, to dress salads, to dip bread. The difference between a decent olive oil and a great one is noticeable in every dish it touches.

This isn't a recipe exactly — it's a guide to making the most of olive oil so you're using it correctly and not wasting what makes it special.

Choosing Olive Oil

  • Extra virgin is the highest quality — pressed cold from fresh olives, unrefined, full flavor. Use this for dressings, dipping, and finishing.
  • Pure or light olive oil is refined and has a higher smoke point. Use this for high-heat cooking like sautéing and roasting.
  • Look for a harvest date on the bottle — fresher is better. Olive oil doesn't get better with age.
  • Store in a cool, dark place away from heat and light. Never next to the stove.

Best Ways to Use It

  1. Dipping oil: Pour good extra virgin olive oil onto a plate, add a pinch of flaky salt, cracked pepper, and a splash of balsamic. Dip crusty bread directly.
  2. Salad dressing: Whisk 3 parts olive oil to 1 part vinegar or lemon juice with salt, pepper, and a small amount of Dijon mustard to emulsify.
  3. Drizzle to finish: A swirl of good olive oil over soup, roasted vegetables, pasta, or hummus right before serving adds richness and flavor you can't get from cooking.
  4. Sautéing: Use light olive oil for medium to medium-high heat cooking — garlic, onions, proteins. Don't let it smoke.
  5. Roasting vegetables: Toss vegetables with olive oil, salt, and pepper before roasting. The oil helps them caramelize and develop flavor in the oven.

Harley's tip: Keep two bottles — an everyday cooking olive oil and one nicer bottle for finishing and dressing. You don't need to cook with your expensive oil. Save it for the moments where the flavor actually shines through raw, like on a salad or drizzled over a bowl of soup.
