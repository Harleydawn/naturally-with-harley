Homemade Chocolate Syrup

By Harley  ·  From My Kitchen

I stopped buying Hershey's the day I realized this takes five minutes and five ingredients I already had in my pantry. It tastes richer, it's cleaner, and it costs almost nothing to make. We drizzle it on ice cream, stir it into milk, put it on pancakes — my family goes through a jar a week and I don't feel one bit guilty about it.

This is one of those recipes that makes you wonder why you ever bought the bottled version in the first place.

What You Need

  • 1 cup unsweetened cocoa powder
  • 1 cup water
  • 1½ cups sugar
  • ¼ tsp salt
  • 1 tsp pure vanilla extract

How to Make It

  1. Whisk the cocoa powder and water together in a small saucepan until smooth with no lumps.
  2. Add the sugar and salt. Place over medium heat, stirring constantly.
  3. Bring to a gentle boil, then reduce heat and let it simmer for 2–3 minutes, still stirring. It will thicken slightly as it cooks.
  4. Remove from heat and stir in the vanilla extract.
  5. Let it cool completely, then pour into a glass jar or squeeze bottle.
  6. Store in the refrigerator. It will thicken more as it chills. Keeps for up to a month.

Harley's tip: If it gets too thick in the fridge, just let the jar sit on the counter for a few minutes before using, or run it under warm water. It loosens right back up. You can also store it on the counter if you go through it fast — it keeps at room temperature for up to two weeks.

That's really it. Five ingredients, one pan, done. No corn syrup, no artificial flavoring — just real cocoa and real sugar the way it's supposed to be. Once you make this you'll never go back to the bottle.
