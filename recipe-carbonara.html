Homemade Spaghetti Carbonara

By Harley  ·  From My Kitchen

Real carbonara has four ingredients — pasta, eggs, cheese, and cured pork. That's it. No cream, no weird shortcuts, no shortcuts at all. The silky sauce comes entirely from the eggs and the starchy pasta water combined off the heat. It's simple once you understand the technique, and once you get it right it's one of the most satisfying things you can put on a plate.

I use regular bacon when I can't find pancetta and it works perfectly. The key is pulling the pan off the heat before you add the egg mixture so it doesn't scramble.

What You Need — Serves 4

  • 1 lb spaghetti
  • 6 oz pancetta or thick-cut bacon, diced
  • 4 large eggs
  • 1 cup finely grated Parmesan or Pecorino Romano
  • 4 garlic cloves, smashed (optional but good)
  • Lots of freshly cracked black pepper
  • Salt for pasta water

How to Make It

  1. Bring a large pot of heavily salted water to a boil. Cook the spaghetti until just al dente — it will finish cooking in the sauce.
  2. While pasta cooks, fry the pancetta or bacon in a large skillet over medium heat until crispy and the fat has rendered, about 8 minutes. Add garlic if using and cook 1 more minute. Remove from heat.
  3. In a bowl, whisk together the eggs and grated cheese until smooth. Season with lots of black pepper.
  4. Reserve 1 cup of pasta cooking water before draining. Drain the pasta.
  5. Add the hot drained pasta directly to the skillet with the pancetta. Toss well so the pasta is coated in the fat. The pan should be OFF the heat now.
  6. Pour the egg and cheese mixture over the pasta and toss quickly, adding pasta water a splash at a time until you have a silky, creamy sauce that coats every strand.
  7. Serve immediately with extra cheese and more black pepper.

Harley's tip: The number one mistake with carbonara is adding the eggs while the pan is too hot — they scramble and you get egg noodles, not sauce. Take the pan completely off the heat, let it sit 30 seconds, then pour in the egg mixture. Toss fast. The residual heat from the pasta does all the work.
