Homemade Worcestershire Sauce

By Harley  ·  From My Kitchen

Worcestershire sauce is one of those ingredients you use in small amounts that makes an enormous difference — in marinades, gravies, stews, burgers, Caesar dressing, and Bloody Marys. The store-bought version is fine, but homemade worcestershire is deeper, more complex, and free of the artificial ingredients in commercial versions.

This version doesn't have anchovies (the traditional ingredient) so it's accessible for everyone and still incredibly savory and bold.

What You Need — Makes about 1 cup

  • ½ cup apple cider vinegar
  • 2 tbsp soy sauce
  • 2 tbsp water
  • 1 tbsp brown sugar
  • ¼ tsp ground ginger
  • ¼ tsp garlic powder
  • ¼ tsp onion powder
  • ¼ tsp ground cloves
  • ¼ tsp cinnamon
  • ¼ tsp black pepper
  • Pinch of cayenne
  • 1 tsp tamarind paste (optional — adds depth)
  • 1 tsp molasses

How to Make It

  1. Combine all ingredients in a small saucepan over medium heat.
  2. Stir to dissolve the sugar and bring to a gentle simmer.
  3. Reduce heat and simmer for 5 minutes, stirring occasionally.
  4. Remove from heat and let cool completely.
  5. Strain through a fine mesh strainer into a jar or bottle.
  6. Refrigerate. Keeps for up to 3 months. Shake before each use.

Harley's tip: The tamarind paste and molasses are both optional, but they're what gives worcestershire sauce its distinctive dark color and deep flavor. If you have them, use them. Even without them this sauce is very good and adds great depth to anything savory.
