Homemade Hoagie Rolls

By Harley  ·  From My Kitchen

Once you make hoagie rolls from scratch, the bagged ones from the store feel like a completely different product. These are soft in the middle, have a slight chew on the outside, and hold up to anything you pile into them without falling apart. My family uses these for sandwiches all week and nobody ever asks to go back to store bread.

They take about two hours start to finish — most of that is just waiting for the dough to rise. The actual hands-on time is maybe twenty minutes.

What You Need — Makes 6 rolls

  • 3 cups bread flour
  • 2¼ tsp active dry yeast (one standard packet)
  • 1 tsp salt
  • 1 tbsp sugar
  • 1 tbsp olive oil
  • 1 cup warm water (around 110°F — warm but not hot)
  • For the top: 1 egg beaten with 1 tbsp water

How to Make It

  1. Dissolve the yeast and sugar in the warm water. Let it sit for 5–10 minutes until it gets foamy on top. If it doesn't foam, your water was too hot or too cold — start over.
  2. In a large bowl, mix together the flour and salt. Pour in the yeast mixture and olive oil and stir until a shaggy dough forms.
  3. Turn out onto a lightly floured surface and knead for 8 minutes until the dough is smooth, soft, and springs back when you poke it.
  4. Place in a lightly oiled bowl, cover with a clean towel, and let rise in a warm spot for 1 hour until doubled in size.
  5. Punch the dough down and divide into 6 equal pieces. Shape each into a long oval roll about 6 inches long.
  6. Place on a parchment-lined baking sheet, cover, and let rise for another 30 minutes.
  7. Preheat your oven to 375°F. Bake for 18–22 minutes until golden brown on top.
  8. Cool on a wire rack for at least 10 minutes before slicing.

Harley's tip: For a slightly crispier crust, put a small pan of hot water on the bottom rack of your oven while the rolls bake. The steam makes the outside beautifully chewy without drying out the inside.

These freeze really well too. Once they're fully cooled, wrap individually and freeze. Pull one out the night before and let it thaw on the counter — it'll taste just as fresh as the day you made it.
