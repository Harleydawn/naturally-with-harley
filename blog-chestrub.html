DIY Chest Rub With Only 3 Ingredients

By Harley  ·  Essential Oils

I used to grab the blue stuff from the drugstore without even thinking about it. It was just what you did when someone got congested. Then I actually read the label and realized I had no idea what half of those ingredients were. That was enough for me to start looking for something better.

This DIY chest rub took me about five minutes to make the first time and I've been using it ever since. Three ingredients, no petroleum, no synthetic fragrance — and it actually works.

What You Need

DIY Chest Rub Recipe:

• 3 tablespoons coconut oil (fractionated or solid)
• 10 drops eucalyptus essential oil
• 6 drops lavender essential oil
• 4 drops cedarwood essential oil

Melt the coconut oil if solid, stir in the essential oils, and pour into a small glass jar. Let it set at room temperature. Apply a small amount to the chest and upper back as needed.

Why These Oils

Eucalyptus is the workhorse here. It contains a compound called cineole that has been studied for its ability to support respiratory function and ease congestion. It's cooling, it opens things up, and the smell alone feels like relief. Cedarwood is grounding and adds an earthy warmth that makes the whole blend feel more like a real product and less like something you threw together in your kitchen — even though that's exactly what it is.

I specifically leave out anything that could cause sensitivity, especially for use around kids. Eucalyptus and cedarwood are both considered safe when diluted properly in a carrier oil like coconut oil.

How I Use It

I rub a small amount on my son's chest and upper back before bed when he's congested. The smell fills the room within a few minutes and it genuinely helps him breathe easier through the night. I use it on myself too when I feel something coming on — a little on my chest before bed and I usually feel better by morning.

Important: Always dilute essential oils before applying to skin. This recipe is already diluted in coconut oil, but use a small amount and do a patch test first, especially on kids.

Storage and Shelf Life

Store it in a small glass jar with a lid. It keeps for several months at room temperature. If your coconut oil is solid, the rub will be firm — just scoop a little out with your finger and it melts on contact with your skin. If it's warm in your house, it may stay more liquid, which works just as well.

One batch lasts me a long time. It costs almost nothing to make and I feel genuinely good about every ingredient in it. That's the whole point.
