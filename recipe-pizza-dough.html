Homemade Pizza Dough

By Harley  ·  From My Kitchen

Friday night pizza is a tradition at our house and this dough is the reason it works. Six ingredients, ten minutes of actual effort, and you get a crust that's crispy on the outside and chewy on the inside. My kids get to pick their own toppings and they love it every single time.

I've tried a lot of pizza dough recipes and this is the one I keep coming back to. Simple, reliable, and so much better than anything from a tube or a box.

What You Need — Makes 2 medium pizzas

  • 3 cups all-purpose flour (bread flour works even better)
  • 2¼ tsp active dry yeast (one packet)
  • 1 tsp salt
  • 1 tsp sugar
  • 2 tbsp olive oil
  • 1 cup warm water

How to Make It

  1. Dissolve the yeast and sugar in the warm water. Let sit 5–10 minutes until foamy.
  2. Mix the flour and salt in a large bowl. Add the yeast mixture and olive oil and stir until a rough dough comes together.
  3. Turn out onto a floured surface and knead for 8 minutes until the dough is smooth and just slightly tacky. It should spring back when you poke it.
  4. Drizzle a little olive oil in the bowl, place the dough in, and turn it to coat. Cover and let rise for 1 hour until doubled.
  5. Punch down and divide in half. Let each piece rest for 10 minutes — this relaxes the gluten and makes stretching so much easier.
  6. On a floured surface, stretch or roll each piece to your desired size and thickness.
  7. Add your toppings and bake at 450°F for 12–15 minutes until the crust is golden and the cheese is bubbling.

Harley's tip: Preheat your baking sheet or pizza stone in the oven before you put the pizza on it. Sliding the topped pizza onto a hot surface is what gives you that crispy bottom crust. Worth the extra step every time.

Honestly, the best thing you can do is make this dough the night before and let it rise slowly in the fridge overnight. Cold-rise dough develops so much more flavor — it makes a noticeable difference and the dough is even easier to stretch. Just pull it out 30 minutes before you're ready to use it so it can come to room temperature. If you have the time, this is the way to go every single time.
