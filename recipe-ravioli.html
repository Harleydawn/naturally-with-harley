Homemade Ravioli

By Harley  ·  From My Kitchen

This is the dinner my family asks for on special nights. It takes more time than most of what I make, but it is so worth it. Fresh pasta, a simple ricotta filling, and a sauce of your choice — there is nothing like it. And honestly, once you've made ravioli from scratch you realize it isn't as hard as it sounds. It's just a little slow and totally satisfying.

I make this on weekend afternoons when I actually have time to enjoy the process. It's become something I look forward to.

For the Pasta Dough — Serves 4

  • 2 cups all-purpose flour
  • 3 large eggs
  • 1 tbsp olive oil
  • ½ tsp salt

For the Filling

  • 1 cup whole milk ricotta cheese
  • ½ cup freshly grated parmesan
  • 1 egg yolk
  • ½ tsp salt
  • ¼ tsp black pepper
  • 1 tbsp fresh parsley, finely chopped

How to Make It

Make the dough:

  1. Mound the flour on a clean surface and make a well in the center. Crack the eggs into the well, add the olive oil and salt.
  2. Beat the eggs with a fork, slowly pulling in flour from the edges of the well until a rough dough forms.
  3. Knead the dough by hand for 10 minutes until it is smooth, firm, and elastic. It should feel like soft Play-Doh.
  4. Wrap in plastic wrap and let rest at room temperature for 30 minutes. This step is important — don't skip it.

Make the filling:

  1. Stir together the ricotta, parmesan, egg yolk, salt, pepper, and parsley until well combined. Set aside.

Assemble and cook:

  1. Divide the dough in half. On a well-floured surface, roll each half as thin as you can get it — nearly translucent. A pasta machine makes this much easier if you have one.
  2. On one sheet of dough, drop teaspoon-sized mounds of filling about 2 inches apart in rows.
  3. Lay the second sheet of dough on top. Gently press around each filling mound to remove air bubbles and seal the dough.
  4. Cut into squares using a sharp knife, pizza cutter, or pasta wheel. Press the edges firmly to seal.
  5. Bring a large pot of salted water to a boil. Cook the ravioli in batches for 3–4 minutes until they float and the pasta is tender.
  6. Serve immediately with marinara, butter and sage, or any sauce you love.

Harley's tip: Press firmly around each filling pocket before you cut. Air bubbles are the enemy — they cause ravioli to burst open in the water. Take your time sealing them and you'll be fine.

Leftover ravioli can be frozen on a baking sheet until solid, then transferred to a freezer bag. Cook straight from frozen — just add 2 extra minutes to the boil time. Future you will be very glad you made extra.
