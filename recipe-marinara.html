Homemade Marinara Sauce

By Harley  ·  From My Kitchen

A good marinara is one of the most useful things in your kitchen. It goes on pasta, pizza, chicken parmesan, meatballs, calzones — anything that needs a bright, garlicky tomato sauce. This recipe is simple, quick, and tastes better than anything from a jar because it's made with whole ingredients and nothing artificial.

Thirty minutes, one pan, real tomatoes. That's all this takes.

What You Need — Makes about 3 cups

  • 1 can (28 oz) whole San Marzano tomatoes (or crushed)
  • 4 garlic cloves, minced
  • ¼ cup olive oil
  • 1 tsp salt
  • ½ tsp black pepper
  • 1 tsp dried oregano
  • ½ tsp red pepper flakes (optional)
  • 6–8 fresh basil leaves
  • Pinch of sugar (optional, to balance acidity)

How to Make It

  1. Heat the olive oil in a saucepan over medium heat. Add the garlic and cook for 1–2 minutes until fragrant — don't let it brown.
  2. Add the tomatoes. If using whole tomatoes, crush them by hand as you add them into the pot.
  3. Add the salt, pepper, oregano, and red pepper flakes. Stir to combine.
  4. Bring to a simmer, then reduce heat to low. Cook uncovered for 20–25 minutes, stirring occasionally, until the sauce thickens.
  5. Tear the fresh basil leaves and stir them in during the last 5 minutes.
  6. Taste and adjust with salt, sugar (if too acidic), and more basil. Keeps in the fridge for up to 1 week, or freeze for 3 months.

Harley's tip: San Marzano tomatoes make a noticeable difference — they're less acidic and more flavorful than regular canned tomatoes. Worth the extra dollar. I make a double batch and freeze half so I always have marinara ready when we need it.
