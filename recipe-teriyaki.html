Homemade Teriyaki Sauce

By Harley  ·  From My Kitchen

Homemade teriyaki sauce comes together in ten minutes and is so much better than the stuff in a bottle — no artificial flavors, no corn syrup, just soy sauce, honey, garlic, and ginger cooked into a glossy, sticky glaze that works on absolutely everything. We use it for chicken, salmon, stir fry, noodles, and rice bowls.

Make a jar and keep it in the fridge. It lasts for weeks.

What You Need — Makes about 1 cup

  • ½ cup soy sauce (or tamari for gluten-free)
  • ¼ cup honey (or brown sugar)
  • 2 tbsp rice vinegar (or apple cider vinegar)
  • 2 tbsp water
  • 3 garlic cloves, minced
  • 1 tsp fresh ginger, grated (or ½ tsp ground)
  • 1 tbsp sesame oil
  • 1 tbsp cornstarch + 2 tbsp cold water (for thickening)
  • Sesame seeds for finishing

How to Make It

  1. Combine soy sauce, honey, rice vinegar, water, garlic, ginger, and sesame oil in a small saucepan over medium heat.
  2. Stir and bring to a gentle simmer.
  3. In a small bowl, whisk the cornstarch with 2 tablespoons of cold water until smooth.
  4. Pour the cornstarch mixture into the simmering sauce while stirring constantly.
  5. Cook for 2–3 minutes until the sauce thickens and becomes glossy.
  6. Remove from heat and cool. It thickens more as it cools. Store in a jar in the fridge for up to 3 weeks.

Harley's tip: For teriyaki chicken, marinate the chicken in half the sauce for at least 30 minutes before cooking. Use the remaining sauce to glaze it during cooking and again right before serving. The double layer of sauce is what gives you that sticky, caramelized restaurant finish.
